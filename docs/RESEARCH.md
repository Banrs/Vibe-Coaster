# Step 0 research report (2026-08-08, research session on the new PC)

This document is the output of PLAN.md's Step 0: a skeptical re-verification of every claim in
the plan's Research digest, plus the POV frame-by-frame video analysis. It supersedes the
digest where they disagree. Methods: three web fact-check agents (≥2 independent sources per
claim), four browser video-analysis agents on real onride POV footage (frame-painted to canvas,
pixel-motion/luma scans — no downloads), all non-Fable (Opus). All user decisions below were
confirmed in-session on 2026-08-08.

**Canonical number set (user decision): RCDB/Wikipedia** (internally consistent, carries the
records). Intamin's own figures conflict on several values and are recorded as footnoted
alternates. No Guinness adjudication page exists for any Falcon's Flight record — all record
language traces to Six Flags/Intamin marketing and enthusiast media.

### Slow-beat inventory
1. **Crest crawl/hold at top of cliff climb: ~2.9 s (forward video) / ~4.0 s (backwards video)** — before the edge traverse, not at the dive lip.
2. Station dwell ≥2 s.
3. NOT slow beats: lift crest (sky artifact), dive lip (accelerates monotonically).

### Per-element g-character (from footage + reviews; qualitative)
- Airtime hills: "consecutive strong airtime" (Intamin), low and closely spaced — ejector-class rhythm section.
- Wave turn: bank rolls on at the crest, unrolls on descent (CGI corroborates).
- Camelback: **trimmed in current operation, still floats on descent** — floater-class crown by design intent, tempered in practice.
- Rim turn: moderate outward roll (~20–35° CGI-derived), wide radius — suspense, not force.
- Dive: pure vertical commitment; the fear is attitude + height, not g.
- Pullouts/launch: the post-tunnel launch is the ride's peak-energy moment.

## 3. Tormenta — POV analysis

Source: JWulfCoasters front-row 4K (`youtube.com/watch?v=AHjk2R4da_I`, 2:17, real, ~0–5%
obstruction, daytime; all pre-opening CGI POVs rejected). Ride dispatch→station ≈ 128 s.

- **Sequence:** curves to lift → chain lift ~32.5 s → **high traverse ~16.5 s at crest speed**
  (long panoramic suspense — doubles the pre-drop window) → pitch-over crawl ~4.1 s → **dead-stop
  hold ~1.7 s** (motion below station noise floor; release is a gradual roll-off, no snap) →
  285 ft beyond-vertical drop (release→bottom 6.3 s) → Immelmann → vertical loop
  (teardrop/clothoid, not circular) → cutback (wraps its own approach + brake shelf) →
  **mid-course block brake ~8 s** (two-stage bite, ~8× speed reduction, crawls ~2 s, never stops)
  → drop-away → **second drop: near-vertical, curving right, unheld, ~3.3 s** → 3 smaller
  whippy inversions (last wraps the final brake run/station) → brakes.
- **Beyond-vertical confirmed visually:** during the hold the wide-angle frame contains zero
  sky/horizon — optical axis pitched past 90°; structure visible "behind" the vertical sightline.
- **Pacing budget:** slow/suspense/transport ≈49%, sustained fast ≈33%, brakes ≈15%. Two-pulse
  structure: 55 s ramp-up → 26 s act one (3 big inversions, 1.6–2.0 s gaps) → hard 8 s reset →
  16 s act two (3 elements, ~0.9 s gaps, lower speed, low to the ground, whippy).
- **Element signatures for the FVD templates:** Immelmann = long steep climb, broad high apex,
  *separate lazy half-roll (~1.6 s) decoupled from the half-loop*, exits lower/opposite.
  Loop = steep pinched-top teardrop. Cutback = *rolls while climbing* (pitch+roll together —
  the diagnostic vs Immelmann/loop's pitch-then-roll), hard diagonal roll-out, returns the way
  it came.
- Inversion count: RCDB says 4; POV saw 6 inversion-class apexes (2 likely overbanks). Unsettled.

**User decision (2026-08-08): the hybrid keeps the plan's exclusion of the mid-course
near-stop + second drop** — one continuous energy arc; Tormenta elements are borrowed
individually. This is now a knowing deviation from how Tormenta actually paces itself.

## 5. Envelope (~2041) — grounding and final numbers

### What ASTM F2291 actually says (public reproduction: Rohde 2024, VDV — author is an ASTM
F24 G-Force Task Group member; corroborated by Cargill/JP Research ASTM slides)
- **+Gz:** 6.0 g (0.2–1 s) → 4.0 g (2–4 s) → 3.0 g (5–11.8 s) → 2.0 g (→40 s) → 1.0 g.
  Push-pull: after ≥3 s of −Gz, brief +Gz cap drops to 5.0 g.
- **−Gz:** −2.0 g @0.2 s → −1.5 g (0.5–4 s) → −1.1 g (≥7 s). "Extended" −2.8 g ≤3 s exists
  (bungee-derived). Binding hazard is ocular, not blackout.
- **±Gy:** ±3.0 g (0.2–1 s) → ±2.0 g sustained. Limiter: clavicle/restraint loading + head
  lateral tilt (curve calibrated to 27° head tilt at a 2 g step).
- **±Gx:** +Gx mirrors +Gz (6/4/3, 2.5 beyond 11.8 s; headrest conditions). −Gx by restraint
  class: −1.5 base / −2.0 OTS / −3.5 prone.
- **Combined axis: pairwise ellipses** (X-Y, X-Z, Y-Z — F2291 Figs 10/11/16), NOT a 3-axis sphere.
- **Onset:** least-squares slope over a 100 ms window. No general limit; 15 g/s is the max
  allowable at SARC proof (5–10 g/s recommended design). **Reversal rule:** two sustained events
  <0.2 s apart → each capped at 50% (0.20 s neuromuscular reaction time — irreducible).
- **Roll rate: NO limit exists in ASTM or EN** — rotational accelerations are neither specified
  nor measured. Convert via a = ω²·D if needed.
- The digest's "ASTM brief baseline ≈ +6/−2 Gz" = the 0.2 s Table 7.1 corners, not a general
  baseline (−Gz is −1.5 g by 0.5 s).
- Measurement chain (F2137): seat accelerometer + **4-pole single-pass 5 Hz Butterworth** — the
  kept toolkit's filter matches practice. The toolkit currently lacks push-pull, reversal, and
  pairwise-ellipse checks — candidates for the rewrite.

### Research credibility verdicts on the stretch (most → least credible)
±8 Gx (documented: +8 Gx tolerated, 10 Gx ≥100 s; but −Gx is weaker — literature supports
−6-ish "well-supported") > −3.0 Gz brief (F2291 already grants −2.8/3 s; vests don't address
the ocular hazard — drop that rationale) > 120°/s roll (unconstrained by any standard;
~0.2–0.4 g at head; validate empirically) > ±4.7 Gy (inside Roth's 3.0–5.0 Gy "adequately
supported" band but thinnest evidence of any axis; ~63° extrapolated head tilt — requires
positive lateral head restraint, ≤1 s, tight jerk control; ~1.6× anything ever fielded) >
25 g/s onset (1.7× today's max allowable; MR seats attenuate transients, not plateaus — frame
as track-frame onset with rider-frame ≤15 g/s) > **+8 Gz (least credible: above the +4.7 Gz
G-LOC floor; F2291's Gz is spine-parallel/body-frame, so seat recline re-projects load into
+Gx rather than raising Gz; recline at 30° buys only ~0.5 g)**.

### FINAL ENVELOPE (user decision, 2026-08-08)
> **≈ +7.0/−3.0 Gz · ±4.7 Gy · ±8.0 Gx · ~25 g/s onset · ~120°/s roll** — duration-structured
> like F2291 (scale the curve shapes above, not flat plateaus).

User modified +Gz from 8.0 → **7.0**; all other axes kept per original plan judgment. The
research caveats above stand as documented risk notes (esp.: +7 Gz is credible only as a
brief, <1–2 s peak; ±4.7 Gy presumes head-restraining vests; 25 g/s presumes MR-seat
attenuation to a lower rider-frame onset).

## 7. Video-analysis method notes (for future sessions)

- In-app browser: the Browser pane must be VISIBLE or screenshots time out ("not compositing").
- Direct screenshots of a YouTube `<video>` element render BLACK — paint the frame to a canvas
  overlay via JS, then screenshot. 4K source can be forced. Canvas is not CORS-tainted here.
- **Contact-sheet grids** (3×3+ frames per canvas) — many frames per screenshot; use grids for
  sweeps, single full frames for measurements. (User directive: don't starve data.)
- Zero-screenshot channel: downsample frames to ~96×54, compute mean luma (finds tunnels/night
  events) and frame-to-frame pixel difference (finds stops, holds, brake bites) — cheap and
  precise for TIMING. Caveats: featureless sky reads as frozen (visually confirm every "stop");
  the metric is depth-confounded and saturates — relative comparisons only, never km/h.
- Precise seek: `v.pause(); v.currentTime=t; await seeked`. Theater mode via `.ytp-size-button`;
  fullscreen and computer-zoom are broken in the pane. Mute the video.
- Cross-validate propulsion/brake claims across two independent videos at the same layout
  position — single-video speed bumps can be scenery artifacts.
- Do NOT prime video agents with spec numbers (biases observation); give them structure, not values.
- One video agent at a time (shared pane). Budget ≈20 screenshots/agent; grids stretch this to
  150+ frames.
