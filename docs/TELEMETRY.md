## 0. Conventions used throughout

**Axis convention (both sources agree):**

| Axis | Name | Sign convention | Value at rest in station |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vertical (V) | seat-back → head ("Gz") | positive = pushed into seat | **+1.00 g** |
| Lateral (L) | side to side ("Gy") | positive = one side (recording-dependent) | 0.00 g |
| Longitudinal (G) | fore/aft ("Gx") | positive = pushed back (accel *or* nose-up climb) | 0.00 g |

Vertical **includes gravity** (1 g at rest). Longitudinal cannot distinguish "launch acceleration"
from "gravity component on a climb" — a steady 20° climb reads ~+0.34 g exactly like a 0.34 g boost.
Both are reported as measured.

**Smoothing:** RideForcesDB values below are an 11-sample (0.22 s) centred moving average at 50 Hz.
This reproduces the site's own published statistics *exactly* (verified against Falcon's Flight:
my −0.986/+3.894 V, −1.347/+1.642 L, −1.886/+2.533 G vs the site's identical figures). Raw
unsmoothed values are noticeably spikier and are quoted separately where relevant.

**"Angle"** (where present) = angle between the rider's vertical axis and the gravity vector.
0° = upright, 90° = on the side / on a vertical track, >90° = inverted. Only available for
recordings flagged `advanced` (those carrying a gravity-vector sequence).

**Airtime band definitions** (RideForcesDB's own, used in its published totals):
floater = +0.25 to −0.25 g · flojector = −0.25 to −0.75 g · ejector = ≤ −0.75 g.

# SOURCE 1 — RideForcesDB (rideforcesdb.com)

Crowd-sourced consumer-IMU recordings. Data pulled from the site's `/getRec?id=` endpoint
(big-endian float32 sequences, 50 Hz) and `/rideInfo?id=` (per-recording statistics).

**Global caveats for every entry in this source:**
- Consumer accelerometers (Apple Watch on a wrist, iPhone in a pocket or hand). Not instrumented
  track data. Mounting slop, wrist/limb motion and body compliance all add noise and can inflate
  short peaks.
- Axis calibration is a user/algorithm-supplied rotation matrix. A device that rotates mid-ride
  smears energy between axes.
- Sub-0.2 s "peaks" in raw data are shock/rattle artefacts, not sustained rider load.
- Ride-window start/end (`stIndex`/`enIndex`) are author-set, so t=0 is not identical across
  recordings of the same ride; relative timing within a recording is reliable.

# SOURCE 2 — YouTube: CoasterTalk, "Falcon's Flight Review"

**Video identity:** *"Falcon's Flight Review. – World's tallest, fastest & longest roller
coaster."* — channel **CoasterTalk**, 9:49 (589.7 s), 2160p available.
URL `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=seNRpi4wP-s`.

**Subject:** the same ride as §1.1 — Falcon's Flight, Six Flags Qiddiya City.

### Telemetry provenance and caveats

- The video is **not** a continuous POV. It is an edited review; POV clips carrying the telemetry
  overlay appear only between roughly **t=100 s and t=360 s** of video time, interleaved with
  b-roll and off-ride shots. POV clips after ~370 s carry no overlay and are captioned
  "Unstabilized camera footage".
- **Two separate ride runs are shown**, distinguishable by the overlay's running min/max values:
  a **front-row** run (camera over the nose) and a **back-row** run (camera behind the seats).
  The video description confirms *"custom telemetry data from the front and back rows."*
- **The telemetry is camera-IMU derived, not phone-accelerometer.** At video t≈195 s the creator
  shows the tool used: **"Telemetry Extractor"**, an app that extracts *GPS, accelerometer and
  gyroscope* data from **Insta360** action cameras. So this is a rigidly-mounted action-camera
  IMU — a different (and generally cleaner) instrument than the wrist-worn Apple Watch in §1.1,
  but still a consumer-grade IMU with its own filtering.
- **No speed readout is present anywhere in the overlay.** Only the three g axes. No speed data
  could be sampled from this source.
- Values below were read visually from frames captured at 1080p and rendered into contact
  sheets. Readings at the second decimal are reliable for the large bottom-right numerics;
  the smaller running min/max markers are ±0.02–0.05 g and a few are marked "≈".
- Video time ≠ ride time. Because clips are cut out of order, an absolute ride timeline cannot be
  reconstructed; instead each reading is tagged with the element visible on screen, and the
  overlay's **running min/max** (which only ever grows) is used to order frames within a run.

### g-character summary — Falcon's Flight per this source

- **Peak vertical for the whole ride: +3.23 g (back row) to +3.65 g (front row).** Both are lower
  than the §1.1 Apple-Watch figure of +3.89 g.
- **Peak longitudinal for the whole ride: +0.87 g (back) to +0.91 g (front)**, corroborated
  independently by the narration ("peak acceleration of 0.9 G"). This is **far below** the
  +2.53 g in the §1.1 recording, and is strong evidence that the §1.1 longitudinal peak is the
  wrist-artefact the unreliability flag warns about. **Treat ~0.9 g as the ride's real launch
  longitudinal magnitude.**
- **Maximum longitudinal deceleration: −1.34 g (front) / −0.93 g (back)** — brakes/trims.
- **Lateral is small throughout: the whole-ride lateral envelope is only −0.79 to +0.66 g**, again
  well inside the §1.1 figure of −1.35/+1.64 g. Both sources agree the ride is laterally quiet.
- **Negative-g:** deepest measured −1.15 g (front row). Sampled unloaded moments: −0.91 g over a
  crest, −0.22 g high above the park, −0.13 g at the camelback apex, −0.05 g in the cliff dive,
  +0.08 g on the return run.
- **The 90° cliff dive is measured at ≈0 g vertical (0.17 g front, −0.05 g back)** with the
  longitudinal reading −0.82 g (front, nose-down on the vertical face). It is an unloading event,
  not a negative-g event.
- Transition rates cannot be derived from this source (frames are sampled at 5–10 s of *video*
  time across cut clips, not continuously).
